## Launch objective

The first month should establish the account as a calm, useful destination for families discovering Muslim baby names by **meaning, gender, Quranic connection, origin, and first letter**. The initial goal is not volume for its own sake. It is to give each of the six public boards a clear purpose, populate them with original pins, and learn which search-led themes earn saves and qualified visits.

Pinterest recommends a 2:3 vertical image, such as **1000 × 1500 px**, for standard image Pins; taller images may be cropped in feeds. Pin titles can be up to 100 characters and descriptions up to 800 characters, with descriptions helping Pinterest determine relevance.[^1] This plan uses three original Pins most weeks, spaced two to three days apart, so the account can prioritize clear, useful creative and review results before increasing cadence.[^2]

## Creative system

Every launch Pin should be an original **1000 × 1500 px PNG** using the Quiet Courtyard system: deep emerald or warm parchment ground, antique-gold rule, Fraunces display headline, DM Sans supporting copy, Amiri for optional Arabic accents, and the emerald-and-gold mark. Keep the visual message to one idea. The on-image headline should stay in the upper two-thirds; use the lower area for a restrained call to action and URL. Do not use unlicensed family photography, claims about popularity, or source language that the landing page does not support.

Use **one new composition per Pin**. Vary the archival device across a gold letter index, courtyard arch, tiled aperture, open name-book page, paper slip, and star motif so the feed is recognizably branded without looking repetitive.

## Review and approval process

1. Create the listed Pin image at 1000 × 1500 px using its creative brief.
2. Check the image, board, title, description, and destination URL against this calendar.
3. The account owner chooses the individual Pin and explicitly approves it for publication or scheduling.
4. After publication, record the live Pin URL and review impressions, saves, outbound clicks, and follows weekly.

This approval step is deliberate: Pinterest’s developer guidance says scheduled publishing tools must require the user to choose each Pin rather than initiating publishing automatically.[^3]

## Weekly measurement routine

Use Pinterest Analytics and Google Analytics together. Pinterest recommends reviewing signals such as saves, impressions, followers, and clicks to understand which ideas resonate.[^2] In Google Analytics, filter the `launch_30d` campaign and compare engaged sessions by `utm_content`. At the end of each week, keep the best-performing pillar in the next week’s schedule and revise only one major creative variable at a time: headline, motif, or destination theme.

| Review window | Pinterest signals | Website signals | Decision rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Days 8–10 | Impressions, saves, profile follows | Sessions from `launch_30d` | Identify the board and visual motif earning the most early saves. |
| Days 17–20 | Saves per impression, outbound clicks | Engaged sessions and shortlist activity | Keep the strongest topic; improve the weaker Pin’s title and opening visual. |
| Days 29–30 | Board-level saves, clicks, followers | Campaign sessions by `utm_content` | Select the three most promising themes for Month 2 and create new, non-duplicative Pin concepts. |

## Approval checklist for each Pin

- The chosen board matches the title and destination page.
- The on-image headline is legible at a small mobile size and contains no unsupported popularity or religious claims.
- The image is a distinct 1000 × 1500 px Quiet Courtyard composition, exported as PNG or JPEG.
- The destination URL loads over HTTPS and retains its campaign tag.
- The title, description, image, destination, and publication time have been individually approved by the account owner.
